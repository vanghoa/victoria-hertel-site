**Key words: perceptual thresholds, environmental sensing, spatial memory**  
<br />
*darkmode* explores how technology perceives and reacts to environmental presence through sensor-based responsiveness. Installed in complete darkness, the work contrasts human sensing with the hypersensitivity of machines and their capacity to detect shifts across a broader spectrum of conditions. As visitors traverse the narrow corridor, their movements prompt quiet interactions—mechanical murmurs, shimmering water, reflected glints—layered over a background of cicadas and koels calling through the dark from aboveground. These subtle responses invite a recalibration of perception, time, space, and our co-existence with other presences.
<br />
By reframing Singapore’s Battlebox, an underground WWII military site, as a space of quiet attention, *darkmode* reflects on the shifting roles of technological infrastructures. Once tied to conflict and control, these systems are reimagined as vessels for conservation, decentralisation, and embodied awareness.
<br />
<br />
**Close-Up [ Refracted Rust ]**  
The glass vessels were hand-blown from recycled window glass and infused with iron oxide to create speckled inclusions. This subtle patina echoes the rust-hued steel beams lining the bunker’s ceiling, forging a quiet dialogue between intervention and site. Embedded into the architecture—its textures, weight, and tones—the installation emerges as an extension of its environment.

**Detail [ Sensor Networks ]**  
Using ESP-NOW, the vessels form a decentralized network, sharing signals to guide visitors through the darkness. When one vessel senses movement, it quietly activates the next, creating a chain of cues that transforms the corridor into a field of gentle navigation and attuned pacing.

**Overview [ Soft Thresholds ]**  
Responding to the cat ladder corridor’s architecture and reverberant acoustics, the work amplifies subtle cues such as light, vibration, and sound to gently guide visitors through darkness. As the passage unfolds, it opens overhead into the presence of nature, reframing technology as a quiet companion, from control toward awareness.
