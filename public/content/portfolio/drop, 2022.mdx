**Key words: temporal materiality, ephemeral marking, organic automation**  
<br />
*Drop* is a kinetic installation featuring a suspended borosilicate glass separatory funnel programmed to release single drops of water at intervals. Each drop lands softly, briefly marking the floor before evaporating into air. The vessel is tethered to a stainless steel wire mounted on a custom-engineered 
ceiling frame, allowing it to hover across a six-metre diameter. Its movement is redirected each time a limit switch is triggered, generating a quiet, randomized trajectory.
<br />
By reconfiguring industrial materials and automated behavior into an act of gentle impermanence, *Drop* invites reflection on presence and disappearance—not as opposites, but as coexisting states. The work draws attention to the fragile choreography of time, where meaning emerges not through permanence, but through subtle repetition, delay, and loss.

**Close-Up [ Drip Drop ]**  
Water drips in carefully tuned intervals, 
each drop tethering at the tip of a 
laboratory funnel before falling. The 
eventual drop is slow, often unpredictable 
in its trajectory, drawing attention to the 
interim moment between suspension and 
descent. As the drops land, they mark the 
floor briefly before vanishing into air as 
brief registers of impermanence.

**Detail [ X-Y Drift ]**  
A custom ceiling frame moves the 
vessel along two perpendicular axes in 
randomized patterns. This shifting motion 
gradually sets the glass funnel swaying, 
turning it into a pendulum. As momentum 
builds, it traces arcs up to six metres 
wide, its movement oscillating between 
drift and sudden swing. Together, motion 
and drip form a system of dispersal based 
partly on control, partly on chance.

**Overview [ Tactical Tension ]**  
Suspended from a ceiling-mounted frame, 
the separatory funnel hovers centimetres 
from the floor, linked by a slender 
stainless steel wire. This five-metre 
span becomes a space of quiet intensity, 
where floor and ceiling are connected in 
a delicate tension. As the glass traces its 
arcs, it fills and empties the air, drawing 
ephemeral lines of presence and pause.
