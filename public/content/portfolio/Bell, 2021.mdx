**Key words: island debris, salt water corrosion, sound trace**  
<br />
*Bell* is a single-unit sound installation that weaves communication points between electro-mechanical, digital, and organic systems. Stepping into its sensorial radius triggers a wired bell to jump upwards, emitting a split-second of sound. This brief auditory occurence serves as an ephemeral point of relation between the bell, the perceiver, and the mediating space.  
<br />
In an effort to avoid single-use plastics in my setups, I opted for a thin stainless steel wire instead of nylon for suspension. This choice not only supports reusability but also enhances the overall resonance of the work, thanks to the inherent material properties of the metal.  
<br />
<br />
**Close-Up [ Corrosion Scars ]**  
I found *Bell* swept up as storm debris on a Singaporean beach. I picked it up 
more for its corrosive aesthetic than any actual expectations of its sound qualities 
remaining intact. But when I gave it a small shake, it still resonated with the 
clearest sound. A sound shaped by ocean currents, salt water erosion, and 
the passage of time.

**Detail [ Pitching Weights ]**  
The characteristic sound of *Bell* is largely defined by its threading. The 
distance between the bell and its two counterweights determines the general 
pitch. Every kink and curve in the stainless steel wire contributes to the 
sound arc it will trace when jolting upward in preparation for ringing. With each 
update to the wire, *Bell* sounds slightly different, allowing it to evolve over time.

**Overview [ Hear Me Now ]**  
Thanks to its compact scale, *Bell* purposefully blends into a space, often causing it to be overlooked. This facilitates an unanticipated jump-start when it suddenly reveals its presence with a sonorous burst. The surprising sound, coupled with the uncertainty of how one’s body triggers the work, enhances our overall spatial awareness.
