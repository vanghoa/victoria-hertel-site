**Key words: observed sensing, molecular profiles, scent trace**  
<br />
*Scent* functions as a real-time visualisation mechanism bridging human and nonhuman materialities. Constructed to refocus our attention on the present moment, the surrounding space, and our presence within it, the installation evolves with our engagement, merging invisible material processes (scent) with visible translations of these (colour).
<br />
The installation features a volatile organic compound (VOC) sensor that artificially mimics the human olfactory system. It detects, measures, and converts scent molecules into scent data. When interacting with the sensor, our molecular inputs are translated into a projected particle system, with our individual aromatic profiles corresponding to a personal set of light gradients and shapes.

<br />
**Close-Up [ Crop Use ]**  
The VOC sensor used in the exhibition 
was originally being researched for 
agricultural purposes. Its intended 
function was to detect changes in the 
volatile organic compound profile of 
crops, alerting farmers when a plant 
showed signs of infection to potentially 
prevent extensive crop loss.

**Detail [ BYO ]**  
 Besides their own presence and scents, 
visitors got creative with the items they 
brought into the space to test on the VOC 
sensor. It converted coffee, flowers, an 
entire olfactory testing kit, and favourite 
perfumes into visuals throughout the 
exhibition.

**Overview [ Particle Spheres ]**  
*Scent* profile visuals were based on 
the molecular density deteced by the 
VOC sensor. High density, indicated by 
lower numbers, produced angular, dark
coloured shapes, while low density, 
indicated by a higher number, resulted in 
circular, light-coloured shapes. Ambient 
air was depicted with small, dust-like 
particles.
