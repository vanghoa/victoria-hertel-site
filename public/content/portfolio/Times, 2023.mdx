**Key words: temporal trace, pitch patina, time-keeping**  
<br />
*Times* is a multi-unit sound installation that delves into the pluralistic nature of time. Approaching the sound structure activates its bell-network, mechanically chiming in soft, unalterable succession— mimicking linear time with each bell struck according to a preset. But when a sensor detects close proximity to a bell-module, a higher pitch surfaces in parallel to the preset. This pitch, unique to our presence near a specific bell, adds a relational quality to time, unfolding multifold from our interactions 
and linking presence with pitch to sonically translate temporal encounters. 
<br />
In sourcing materials, I opt for second-hand items, sustainably repurposing them to contribute to the character of the work. I feel that metal vessels with patina, especially in sound works, offer resonant qualities that newer counterparts have yet to develop.

**Close-Up [ Chime Markings ]**  
The twelve brass chimes in *Times* were all 
sourced from second-hand metronomes, 
collected from individual sellers across 
Singapore in a single day. Exposed to 
diverse wear and tear circumstances, 
each bell was uniquely marked by time. 
These distinct temporal traces led to the 
development of a pitch-altering patina 
that gave the chimes distinctive sound 
qualities.

**Detail [ Response Ringing ]**  
*Times* operates on a controller/device 
system that copies and communicates 
set interactions. It uses of Time-of-Flight 
sensors to measure distances to a grid 
of zones, similar to a spatial chessboard, 
triggering a specific bell to ring when 
someone or something enters its grid. 
By disentangling the metronome bells 
from their original pace-keeping purpose, 
they now ring in response rather than 
repetition.

**Overview [ Time-Sharing ]**  
The open circular layout of *Times* 
was conceptualised for individual and 
collective interweaving. I constructed it 
so that visitors, whether experiencing 
the work alone or in a group, could 
feel a sense of connection through the 
unfolding of sounds over time. Turning 
time-keeping into an act of awareness.
