**Key words: digital shrines, techno-spiritualism, material afterlives**  
<br />
*Tides* deepens my exploration of overconsumption’s environmental impact, first captured through photographs of washed-ashore items on Singapore’s East Coast Park in 2021. This iteration of *Tides* features a crystalline ‘nicho’—a Latin American folk art shrine commemorating events and spiritual connections—crafted from marine pollution glass fragments collected from the same shores. Inside, a sensor-driven custom printed circuit board tea light dims and brightens with visitors’ presence, honouring the cyclical ebb and flow of nature. Merging cultural expression with tech-mediation, this mini ocean shrine reflects on spiritual rites, presence, and memory, paying homage to ecological rhythms.
<br />
*Tides* consists entirely of marine pollution glass collected with the local cleanup group SG Beach Warriors. With rising pollution, upcycling renewable materials like glass is essential for ecological balance.

**Close-Up [ Glass ≠ Glass ]**  
Each bottle, jar, block, and unclassifiable 
fragment used in *Tides* had a distinct glass 
composition. Without knowing the exact 
type of glass, I had to experiment with 
fusing them. Variations in composition, 
shard size, and layering within the mold 
created tensions during and after fusing, 
often resulting in cracks and breakages 
before achieving a stable final piece.

**Detail [ Tech Votive ]**  
The nicho remained unlit in the absence 
of visitors. Only when the time-of-flight 
sensor detected a presence nearby did 
it activate its RGB LED, casting hues 
of cyan blue. Acting as a tech-based 
votive candle, this signaled the shrine’s 
activation, illuminating the fused glass 
and reflecting light around the translucent 
nicho and its surrounding space, 
welcoming the visitor.

**Overview [ Sacred Spaces ]**  
Situated in a narrow gallery corner, the 
space allowed only one or two people 
to approach the ocean nicho at a time. 
This created an intimate moment with 
the piece, fitting the theme of a shrine—a 
place for recollection, remembrance, and 
honoring, often found in spiritual settings.
